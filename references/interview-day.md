# Interview day

Keep messages short. The candidate is nervous; give one thing at a time.

## 2 hours before

- One light mock (10 min) or say the pitch + 3 key answers out loud. No new material.
- Re-read `notes.md`: fixed lines, salary line, "On the record" for this company.
- Check the setup: link opens, camera, mic, headphones, light in front (not behind), plain background, laptop charged, phone silent, notifications off.
- Captions: turning on the meeting tool's built-in captions is fine if it helps with accents.
- Water nearby. Eat something.

## 30 minutes before

- Open `notes.md` on the side, zoomed so keywords are readable at a glance. A paper sheet with topics is fine. No full scripts.
- Read the 3 research facts and the 3 questions to ask.
- Say the opening line out loud: "Hi <name>, nice to meet you. Thanks for taking the time."
- Breathing: 4 seconds in, 6 out, five times.

## 15 minutes before

- Join the waiting room or test the link.
- Stop reading. Warm up the voice: talk out loud for 2 minutes about anything in the interview language.
- Remind them: short sentences, main point first, pauses are fine.

## During

- It's fine to say "I'm a bit nervous, but happy to be here" at the start. It lowers the pressure; don't repeat it.
- If you don't understand: "Sorry, could you rephrase that?"
- If you don't know: say so, then give the closest real experience.
- Take short notes of names, next steps, numbers they mention.

## Salary playbook (quick)

- Give your number calmly. Then stop talking.
- First pushback: "I understand. Based on the scope and my experience, I'm comfortable at X. I'm open to discussing the full package."
- Asked for current salary: "I'd rather focus on what this role is worth. My expectation is X." Never lie about it.
- Don't accept a lower number on the call. "Let me think about it and get back to you" is a complete answer.

## Right after

- Write down everything you remember in 5 minutes: questions, your answers, numbers you said, next steps, names.
- Paste it (or the transcript) into the skill for the debrief.
