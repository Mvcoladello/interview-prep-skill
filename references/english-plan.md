# English plan

The bottleneck for most non-native candidates is **speaking under pressure**, not vocabulary or listening. Train speaking, measure with mocks, and attack the candidate's own recurring mistakes.

## Sizing

| Time left | Daily plan |
|---|---|
| Under 24h | One fluency drill (15 min) + say the pitch and 5 key answers out loud twice. No new grammar. |
| 1–3 days | Fluency drill daily (20 min) + one mock per day + 10 correct sentences for each of yesterday's top 2 mistakes. |
| 4+ days | The standard day below, scaled to the hours available. |
| 2+ weeks | Weekly focus below, plus the standard day. |

## Standard day (scale to the candidate's hours)

| Block | Share | What |
|---|---|---|
| Shadowing | 10% | A 3–5 min tech talk or podcast clip. Repeat sentence by sentence copying rhythm and intonation. Record the last pass. |
| Speaking gym | 25% | Fluency drill prompt (mock-prompts.md). |
| Targeted mistakes | 15% | For each top mistake from yesterday, say 10 correct sentences out loud. |
| Technical English | 25% | 3 questions from the bank: explain out loud, record, transcribe, ask for 2 corrections only. |
| Writing | 10% | A short email, PR summary or design note. Get it corrected and rewrite it. |
| Review | 15% | Flashcards with the day's new phrases (max 20 new). |

Light time (optional): podcasts and series in English without subtitles in the native language.

## Weekly focus (longer preps)

1. **Clean the mistakes**: past tense, short sentences, numbers (prices, percentages, dates, years).
2. **Fluency under pressure**: surprise questions, 60 seconds, no preparation.
3. **Range and nuance**: phrasal verbs, hedging ("I'd argue", "it depends on"), meeting language (disagree, interrupt, summarize). Debate drill.
4. **Performance**: one full mock a day plus feedback; explain a project in 5 minutes like a demo.

## Common mistakes from Portuguese and Spanish speakers

Use these to seed the candidate's own list in `profile.md`; replace them with real ones after the first mock.

| Wrong | Right | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| "I made a research" | "I did some research" | do research, make a decision |
| "explain me" | "explain it to me" | explain **to** someone |
| "I have 6 years" | "I have 6 years of experience" | years of experience |
| "I'm working there since 2021" | "I've been working there since 2021" | present perfect with since/for |
| "It was excited" | "It was exciting" / "I was excited" | -ing = the thing, -ed = the person |
| "the people was" | "the people were" | people is plural |
| "I work in this project last year" | "I worked on this project last year" | past event → past tense; work **on** a project |
| "Depends" | "It depends on..." | full subject + preposition |
| "Actually" meaning "currently" | "Currently" / "right now" | false friend |
| "seven thousand" for seventy | "SEV-en-ty" | stress the first syllable; pause between number words |

## Rescue phrases

- "Let me think for a second."
- "The word escapes me, but basically..."
- "Sorry, could you repeat that?" / "Could you rephrase that?"
- "Let me give you a concrete example."
- "To sum up, ..."

A pause is better than "uh, um".

## Measuring

Track fluency and content per mock in `roadmap.md`. Targets: fluency ≥ 7 for an HR screen, ≥ 8 for client-facing roles. Certificates (EF SET, etc.) measure reading and listening, not speaking; never claim a higher level than the certificate in the interview.
